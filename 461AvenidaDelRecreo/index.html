THE BIG PICTURE

Preserve what makes it home.
Invest in what comes next.

Take care of necessary repairs before they become larger issues, attract long-term renters, and improve the property’s value.

The property pairs the main house—with three bedrooms, one bath, a large living room, and a laundry room—with a separate one-bedroom, one-bath cottage. The plan prioritizes the main house, inside and out. You’ll continue living here while the cottage remains rented, then move to the cottage when the main house is ready to rent—aiming to minimize gaps in rental income.

Potential returns from the supplied proposal, shown separately by improvement. These are planning assumptions, not appraisals or guaranteed results. Cost recovery at sale is different from net profit.

Energy, rental & valuation assumptions +

Windows: the source claims a 25% decrease in energy use and a six-year payback. It also says windows account for roughly 25% of heating and cooling bills. Its illustrative $3,000/year HVAC baseline produces $750/year in utility savings; neither usage nor savings is measured for this property. The six-year payback is a source claim, not calculated from this $18,000 package.

The source also models a $10,000 HVAC system over 15 years (approximately $666/year depreciation), with a three-year life extension and approximately $2,000 in long-term asset savings. If tenants pay utilities, utility savings do not directly become owner income. Review potential incentives and rebates separately.

Bathroom renovation: 60% resale cost recovery, $200+ in potential monthly rental uplift, and a two-to-four-year payback are distinct source assumptions. The payback has not been recalculated for each package.

Complete-property repaint: the source claims 65% resale cost recovery, 2–5% property value uplift, and $150+ monthly rental uplift. Exact return depends on rental length and sale timing. These measures may overlap and are not added together. Main-house paint selections do not confirm complete-property or cottage coverage.

Bathroom addition: the source assumes a 10% increase in rental-price capacity. Its home-value increase is written as “X%,” so no home-value return is calculated.

Property reference: owner-supplied Zillow figures are $1,094,000 Zestimate®, $761/sq ft, $4,278 estimated monthly rent, and $7,303 estimated monthly refinance payment. They have not been independently verified or treated as project outcomes. The property is described as a single-family home built in 1940 on 0.69 acres.
