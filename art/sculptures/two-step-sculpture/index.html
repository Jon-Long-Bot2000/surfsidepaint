Two-Step Sculpture

A kinetic fountain in two independent mechanisms. Water separates a nested cup and bowl, then flows through four spiral aqueducts into four connected cones that descend and spin along the lower screw stand.

Motion study

Two-Step Sculpture · First studyPrescribed motion
A nested cup fills and overflows into a bowl. The bowl descends as the cup rises. Fixed jets reach four spiral channels and storage cones. At the top, a contact-operated valve discharges cup water into the bowl. The bowl drains and rises, lowering the upright cup; its valve closes immediately when top contact ends.

Prescribed 40-second motion study, not a physics simulation. Each vessel has a five-foot target stroke. The cup may return with water remaining. Four cones share a ring and central sleeve. They descend and rotate on the lower screw, then rise with reverse rotation under a proposed counterweight reset. Use the stage buttons or keyboard-accessible timeline to pause and inspect. Reading the fountain

M01 · Cup and bowl

The hollow stand P01 supplies four fixed emitters P07, one foot above the resting cup rim. Cup P03 overflows into bowl P04. The bowl descends and pulls the cup upward through cord P05 over pulley P06. The fixed jets then reach the aqueducts.

At the top, contact P13 opens cup valve P14. Water falls mostly into the bowl, which continues draining through P12. The valve closes on departure; the upright cup returns with some water remaining.

M02 · Chandelier storage

Four fixed, open-top spiral aqueducts P08 feed four equally spaced cone-shaped stores P15 below their outlets at rest. Once the cones move away, this preview sends channel water straight to the basin; intermittent recapture is not modeled. Restricted outlets P16 drain toward catch basin P09; pump P10 recirculates the water.

The cones are joined by a circular ring connected to a sleeve around the lower screw stand. As the loaded assembly descends it rotates; the channels remain fixed. A proposed guided counterweight beside the stand lifts the draining assembly back up with reverse rotation. A separate lift collar and thrust bearing keep the counterweight cord from rotating with the sleeve. No forces, flow rates, water conservation, or physical activation threshold are calculated.
